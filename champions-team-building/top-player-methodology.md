# Top-Player Methodology — Worlds 2026 Case Studies

Extracted from two full team reports by top-level players, both Reg M-B, both with
complete spreads and stated reasoning. Where the two players converge independently,
treat it as a strong signal. Where they diverge, it's a real choice, not a mistake.

- **Wolfe Glick** — 59th at Worlds 2026, 5-3 Day 1. `pokepast.es/508520a77ae639eb`
  Mega Staraptor / Farigiraf / Gholdengo / Tyranitar / Whimsicott / Excadrill
- **Markus Hamann, Rahim Farzan, Jannik Koch, Florian** — one team, three players, **all three made Day 2**.
  `pokepast.es/d4b7bb060ebb6f56`
  Mega Staraptor / Sylveon / Arcanine-Hisui / Kingambit / Basculegion-M / Mega Raichu Y

---

## 1. Convergent evidence (both arrived here independently)

### Mega Staraptor: max Speed, ~zero Attack, everything else bulk
| | HP | Atk | Def | SpA | SpD | Spe | Nature |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wolfe | 31 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 3 | 32 | Jolly |
| Markus | 29 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 4 | 32 | Jolly |

Same four moves (Brave Bird / Close Combat / Tailwind / Protect), same nature, near-identical
point split. Both state the reasoning in the same terms:
- **Speed is non-negotiable.** Wolfe: losing the mirror, Mega Metagross and Alolan Ninetales
  is "a huge weakness." Markus: Timid Floette, Jolly Garchomp, and at worst a mirror speed tie.
- **Attack is nearly free to cut.** Both say Close Combat and Brave Bird "hit hard even without
  attack investment." Wolfe: "While many Staraptor choose to drop some bulk for attack, I felt
  that on my team the most important thing was for Staraptor to survive."
- **Contrary Close Combat is a defensive move.** Markus: "Close Combat's boosts to Defense and
  Special Defense can still be significant and make Staraptor hard to break." It's a
  bulk-accumulating attack, which is why cutting Attack for HP compounds.

### Chople Berry + Low Kick on the 4x-Fighting-weak slot
- Wolfe: **Tyranitar** (Rock/Dark) @ Chople Berry, Low Kick. Explicitly instead of its Mega Stone.
  "Chople Berry meant Tyranitar could solo Chople Berry and Focus Sash Kingambit."
- Markus: **Kingambit** (Dark/Steel) @ Chople Berry, Low Kick. Explicitly instead of "the more
  common Swords Dance set on similar teams."

Both put Chople on a Pokémon that is 4x weak to Fighting so it survives the Fighting move and
wins the trade, and both add Low Kick to beat the mirror. The convention (Mega Stone / Swords
Dance) lost to a matchup-specific item on both teams.

### Offense invested to the benchmark, never past it
Wolfe's six: 0 / 26 / 17 / 5 / 32 / 15 in the relevant offensive stat.
Markus's six: 1 / 20 / 32 / 25 / 28 / **0**.

Markus's Mega Raichu Y runs **zero Special Attack** (32 HP / 11 Def / 23 Spe) because its job is
Zap Cannon's guaranteed paralysis and Fake Out, not damage. Wolfe explicitly notes his team runs
"less offense than the standard sets" and compensates with Sand chip.

**Only one of the twelve Pokémon has a maxed offensive stat plus maxed Speed** (Wolfe's
Whimsicott, 2/0/0/32/0/32). Everything else is bespoke.

### Both anticipate the counterplay to their own plan
- Wolfe identified that teams beat Tailwind with "priority and bulk," then added **Farigiraf's
  Armor Tail** specifically to kill that counterplay and protect a low-HP Staraptor.
- Markus added **Hisuian Arcanine's Extreme Speed** because its +2 priority "allows it to move
  even before Prankster moves, making it possible to stop Tailwind from going up a second time."

Neither stopped at "my plan is good." Both built the answer to the answer.

### Both reversed an initial dismissal
- Wolfe wrote Whimsicott off ("I already had a Tailwind attacker"), retested it, and it became his
  **default fourth pick**. His early Staraptor teams "didn't feel very good" until he swapped
  Roost for Tailwind.
- Markus: "When Mega-Raichu-Y was added... at first I was a bit skeptical."

The first read on a Pokémon is frequently wrong, and the failure is often one moveslot or one
support piece away from being the best thing on the team.

### Neither team has healing
Markus states it outright. Wolfe's only sustain is a Sitrus Berry. Both substitute **bulk plus
turn-order control** for recovery.

## 2. The redundancy rule (the biggest single takeaway)

You bring **4 of 6**. So a second answer to the same threat is not waste — it is what guarantees
the answer is *available* in the games where the first one is matched up badly or already dead.

> Wolfe: "I felt it was important to have multiple answers to Kingambit due to its popularity and
> strength. With Tyranitar, Staraptor, and Gholdengo I found I had **two answers to nearly every
> popular Pokemon in the game** (except for the fairy types), and this meant I could always bring
> two Pokemon that countered my opponents main threats to a match."

Wolfe's layered redundancy:
- Kingambit: Staraptor (Close Combat), Tyranitar (Low Kick)
- Charizard: Gholdengo (Power Gem), Tyranitar
- Tailwind: Staraptor, Whimsicott (Prankster)
- Moving first vs Rock Slide users: Tailwind, Sand Rush
- Speed modes: Tailwind and Trick Room

Markus's: Arcanine and Sylveon "cover each other's weaknesses"; their own Basculegion is their
Basculegion answer; Lightning Rod Raichu covers Archaludon *and* the Raichu mirror.

**Test to apply: for each of the top ~10 threats, name two members that beat it. A threat with one
answer is a threat you lose to whenever that answer is unavailable.**

## 3. Every slot must be able to win a game

Wolfe on Farigiraf: "I knew my team wouldn't be able to dedicate too many resources to a Trick
Room mode, meaning using Farigiraf as a purely defensive Pokemon wouldn't work. I decided to try
Nasty Plot bulky Farigiraf... After a Nasty Plot Farigiraf outputs huge damage into nearly every
Pokemon in the game, **making Farigiraf a standalone threat**."

He says it about Staraptor too: supportive (Intimidate + Tailwind), offensive (Close Combat +
Brave Bird), or setup (Contrary + Charm). Three modes, one slot.

Markus's Raichu is the same idea from the other direction: it functions **un-megaed** (Lightning
Rod) or megaed (No Guard Zap Cannon), so the slot is live either way.

**Test: if a slot only does its job in one mode, or only when the game is going well, it is too
narrow for a 6-slot team.**

## 4. The Mega Stone is an option, not an obligation

- Wolfe **gave up Tyranitarite entirely** for a Chople Berry, having decided Staraptor was coming
  to nearly every game anyway.
- Markus deliberately **delays** Mega Evolution on both of his:
  - Raichu: stays base to keep **Lightning Rod**, and because No Guard also lets the *opponent*
    never miss against Raichu. Its bulk is identical in both forms, so there is no cost to waiting.
  - Staraptor: delaying keeps the opponent guessing which of the two Megas you actually brought.

**Two Megas on a team is not just two modes — it's hidden information.** Holding the stone
unexercised is itself a resource.

## 5. Spreads are derived, and optimized to the single point

Every number in both reports is justified by a named calc against a **named opposing spread** —
not "survives Kingambit" but "survives +1 Adamant Life Orb Max Attack Kingambit Sucker Punch."

### Single-point residual optimization (verified against the formulas)
| | points | HP stat | effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wolfe's Staraptor | 31 HP | **191** | Sandstorm (1/16) deals 11 |
| | 32 HP | 192 | Sandstorm deals **12** |
| Wolfe's Gholdengo | 7 HP | **169** | Life Orb (1/10) recoil 16 |
| | 8 HP | 170 | recoil **17** |

Both confirmed exactly. Markus does the same on Arcanine: "The 1 HP and 1 SpD investment optimize
rolls against Mega-Floette Light of Ruin + Whimsicott Moonblast in Fairy Aura."

### Leftover points still get a reason
Markus's Sylveon: "We put the remaining 2 points in speed, because we wanted to feel more
confident against opposing Sylveon while also potentially outspeeding Pokémon like Mega Raichu-Y
and Delphox that go just barely above Jolly Sneasler."

### They quantify to the roll and accept probabilities
"15/16 times." "around an 85% chance to 2HKO." "most of the time." "around a 50/50 roll."
A named probability is an acceptable answer; a vague one is not.

### Spread shapes
Wolfe's Excadrill invests in **five of six stats** (12/15/13/9/17 with 0 SpA) and maxes none.
Markus's Basculegion invests in three and maxes none. Clean max/max spreads are the exception.

## 6. Reliability is a first-class variable

- Wolfe picked **Power Gem** over stronger options because it is "a 100% accurate rock type
  special move" that "will always KO Charizard in just one hit." He rejected Dual Wingbeat because
  it "is significantly weaker and can miss," and chose High Horsepower because it "doesn't hit
  your partner."
- Markus built around **No Guard Zap Cannon** — an ability that converts a 50%-accuracy move into
  a 100% guaranteed paralysis. That's the whole reason Raichu is on the team.
- Markus also accepts an inaccuracy *deliberately* and says so: Head Smash's accuracy "is an
  issue, but we've decided it was necessary to live with this inconsistency."

Decide accuracy explicitly, in either direction. Don't leave it implicit.

---

## 7. Exploit the fact that the whole metagame EVs against shared benchmarks

Wolfe's Expert Belt Excadrill is the sharpest idea in either report:

> "These Pokemon will sometimes train themselves to survive Kingambit Iron Head, **which is the
> same as Excadrill Iron Head**. With Expert Belt Excadrill becomes even stronger, and effectively
> always picks up the KO. This is very relevant against bulky Calm Mind Floette, who can be a
> problem if it survives the Iron Head."

Opponents EV against the *popular* user of a move. Bring the unpopular user with a damage item and
their benchmark is wrong.

Related: Wolfe uses **Sand Stream offensively** — as chip to convert rolls into guaranteed KOs,
precisely *because* his attackers run less offense than standard. A "defensive" ability doing
offensive work.

## 8. Speed control is decided first, and stacked

Wolfe: "I wanted to heavily prioritize speed control from the beginning due to the volatile nature
of Regulation M-B, **especially having lost NAIC to Rock Slide flinches**." The motivation is
variance reduction, not tempo.

- **Wolfe, 4 layers:** Tailwind (Staraptor), Prankster Tailwind (Whimsicott), Trick Room
  (Farigiraf), Sand Rush (Excadrill).
- **Markus, 4 layers:** Tailwind (Staraptor), guaranteed paralysis (Zap Cannon), Fake Out (Raichu),
  and an explicit **"priority spam"** package — "Sucker Punch, Quick Attack, Extreme Speed and Aqua
  Jet have surprisingly solid coverage, while guaranteeing a lot of combo KOs."

Markus's priority package is a **second, orthogonal engine**: it lets the team attack first with no
speed control up at all. Note also that paralysis is speed control that *halves them* rather than
doubling you — it survives your own setter fainting.

### Correction to a prior assumption in this pack
Wolfe's Worlds team runs **no Fake Out and no redirection at all**. Markus's runs one Fake Out and
no redirection. Their support is entirely **turn-order and stat manipulation** (Intimidate,
Tailwind, Trick Room, Armor Tail, Sand, Encore, Charm, paralysis) rather than action denial.
Treat "you need Fake Out" as a soft heuristic; "you need redundant turn-order control" is the
harder rule.

---

## 9. The team isn't done when the six are chosen

Both reports ship a **matchup spreadsheet with per-matchup game plans**. Both name their default
four. Wolfe tracked bring rates across the tournament:
- Staraptor: "my only Pokemon who ended up coming to every single match"
- Excadrill: "the Pokemon I used the least, it was very strong nonetheless"

A low-usage slot that wins specific matchups is fine and intended. The deliverable is six
Pokémon **plus the bring-4 decision tree**.

Both also separate team quality from result. Wolfe: "This team was strong enough to win the World
Championships - I can say this with full confidence... Pokemon is a game where a lot of factors
are out of your control."

## 10. Champions facts confirmed in play by these reports

- **Mega Staraptor** — Contrary + Flying/Fighting. Close Combat raises Def and SpD.
- **Mega Raichu Y** — No Guard. **Zap Cannon becomes 100% accurate → guaranteed paralysis.**
  Raichu's bulk is *identical* base and mega, so delaying Mega costs nothing defensively.
  No Guard also lets opponents never miss against it.
- **Mega Raichu Y is outsped by Aerodactyl and Delphox** (Markus), which is why it went support.
- **Hisuian Arcanine** — Rock Head (recoil-free Head Smash) + Extreme Speed at +2 priority,
  which moves before Prankster. OHKOs any Charizard with Head Smash and outspeeds Modest ones.
- **Fairy Aura boosts Whimsicott's Moonblast** — both players EV against "Moonblast in Fairy Aura,"
  confirming the field-wide, both-sides behaviour.
- **Basculegion-M** — Adaptability, Last Respects as an endgame move, Aqua Jet, and a Water
  resistance that makes it a reliable Sneasler switch-in.
- **Gholdengo Power Gem** — 100% accurate, and with Life Orb always OHKOs Charizard. Good as Gold
  blanks Sleep Powder, Encore and Perish Song.
- **Pelipper in Champions runs only Weather Ball and Hurricane** (Wolfe), so it's always safe to
  switch into.
- Meta note (M-B): Kingambit sets with **only Dark moves + Swords Dance** were a trend; Tyranitar
  crushes those with its Dark resistance and base 150 Def mega.
